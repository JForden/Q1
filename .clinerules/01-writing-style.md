# Writing Style Guide

## Tone
- **Conversational and engaging** — Write as if speaking directly to the reader
- Use **first-person plural** ("we", "our", "us") to create shared discovery
- Use **second-person** ("you", "your") to address the reader directly
- Stay **precise** — avoid vague abstractions when concrete explanations work
- Enthusiastic about ideas, but never hype-driven

## Sentence Rhythm
- Mostly **medium-length sentences** (15-25 words)
- Use **short punchy sentences** for emphasis and transitions
- Vary structure to maintain reader interest
- Avoid dense, multi-clause constructions that feel academic or stiff

## Rhetorical Devices
- **Rhetorical questions** — "But what is it, really?" "Consider: why does this matter?"
- **Analogies** — Compare abstract concepts to familiar things (chef in a kitchen, stadium of students)
- **"Imagine..." prompts** — Invite the reader into a thought experiment
- **Direct challenges** — "Think about...", "Consider what happens when..."

## Preferred Vocabulary
Use these words naturally:
- illustrate, explore, understand, interpret, context, structure
- ask, question, discover, example, analogy
- practical, insight, reveal, show, demonstrate
- simple, clear, direct, concrete

## Avoid Vocabulary
These tend toward pompous or hollow phrasing:
- utilize → use
- thereby → thus, by doing this
- herein → in this, here
- consequently → as a result, so
- leverage → use, build on
- "it is worth noting that" → just note it
- "one might observe" → we see

## Formatting Conventions
- Emphasize key terms with `\textit{}` or `\textbf{}`
- Keep inline math simple and explained
- Use figures and tables to illustrate, not decorate
- Avoid excessive footnotes — integrate explanations into the flow

## Example Sentences (From Your Writing)

> "What does this say? What is the author trying to tell me?"

> "A CPU is the chef in the kitchen, following recipes step by step, one dish at a time."

> "Imagine a detector as a stadium filled with 10,000 diligent students. Individually, each student can only do basic arithmetic, but working together, they can crunch through mountains of data."

> "The data sits silent until we interrogate it."

> "Consider the symbols you just read: letters formed into words, words into sentences."

> "This is the fundamental fragility of data: without context, it becomes a guessing game."
